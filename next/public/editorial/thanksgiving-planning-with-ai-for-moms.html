Moms, if you’re already feeling the holiday stress, you’re not alone. In a 2025 survey of 1,000 moms by Calm, 65% said the pressure to make the holidays perfect feels overwhelming, and 59% named the mental load of planning and scheduling as a stress trigger.

The cooking is only part of the Thanksgiving load. The rest sits in the back of your head at 10pm: who hasn’t answered the group text, whether your sister-in-law is still dairy-free, if there are enough chairs, and how the stuffing and the sweet potatoes are going to share one oven.

How Can Moms Use AI to Plan Thanksgiving?

The short answer: give Claude one detailed prompt that interviews you first, then plans in order: the guest list, the menu and quantities, the turkey, the shopping, the make-ahead plan, the oven schedule, the kids, the table, your Thanksgiving Day timeline and the day after. You answer the questions. Claude does the lists, the math and the timeline, and you make every call.

What Is the Full ai.moms™ Thanksgiving Method Prompt?

Here’s the ai.moms™ Thanksgiving Method in RCTF form: Role, Context, Task, Format. The prompt covers the same 13 steps as the table above, written so you can paste them straight into Claude.

The ai.moms™ Thanksgiving Method

Role: You are my Thanksgiving planner. Take the whole thing off my plate, from today through the day after, and build the plan around my family, my kitchen, my budget and my time, so that on the day I actually get to enjoy it.

Context: We’re [insert hosting, going to someone else’s house or splitting the day] on Thursday, November 26, and want to eat at [insert time]. Before you plan anything, interview me in small groups of 2 or 3 questions, one topic at a time, and wait for my answers. Ask about: who’s coming (adults, kids and their ages, who’s staying over, who hasn’t answered); allergies and diets; our non-negotiable dishes and traditions; my ovens, burners, appliances, fridge and counter space; our table, chairs and space; what equipment I have and what I’m missing; my budget and how much I want to cook versus buy or have people bring; who’s helping and who always offers but never follows through; what stressed me out last time; how I want to feel this year; and anything else on my plate that week. If I say “skip” or “not sure,” suggest a sensible default and move on. Keep a running list of everything I tell you and use it in every step.

Task: Work through these steps in order. After each one, show me what you made and ask if I want changes before you move on. 1) A summary of our Thanksgiving for me to confirm. 2) The guest list with who’s bringing what and allergies, a warm invite text and a follow-up for anyone who hasn’t answered. 3) The menu with exact quantities for our headcount plus a little extra for leftovers, who makes each dish, allergy swaps, and a message telling each guest what to bring and how much. 4) The turkey: fresh or frozen, size, where and when to order, the day to start thawing and a backup plan. 5) One shopping list by store section, split into three trips (now, the weekend before, two days before), with a running total. 6) A make-ahead plan, a fridge plan for the night before, and a serving dish and spoon for every dish. 7) An oven and stove schedule worked backwards from mealtime, so everything comes out hot together. 8) Kids’ activities by age, small jobs and a supply list. 9) Table setup, a seating chart idea, simple decorations and one gratitude moment. 10) A playlist plan and a self-serve drinks station. 11) An hour-by-hour timeline for the day before and Thanksgiving Day (when each dish goes in the oven, when guests arrive, when we eat), with a job and a short message for each helper, and a 15-minute break for me. 12) Quick fixes for what usually goes wrong. 13) A cleanup plan, leftovers to go, three easy leftover meals, a thank-you text and a note for next year. Use today’s date to count how many days I have. Search the web for current turkey prices and order deadlines at my stores, delivery slots and recipes. Follow USDA food safety guidance: thaw a frozen turkey in the fridge (about 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds), cook it to 165°F in the thickest part of the thigh, breast and stuffing, and get leftovers in the fridge within 2 hours.

Format: Finish with one clean plan organized by date, from today through the day after. Then ask if I want the key dates added to my calendar (order the turkey, start thawing, each shopping trip, set the table, start cooking) and if I want any part turned into a checklist I can print or keep on my phone.

Before you start, ask me any clarifying questions.

What Should Moms Double-Check Before Acting on the Plan?

AI does the planning. You still make the call, especially on food safety and money.

  • Use a meat thermometer. A cook time from a chat is a starting point. The 165°F reading is the finish line.
  • Check every price, deadline and delivery slot on the store’s own site before you count on it.
  • Confirm allergies with the guest. Text them the swap Claude suggested and get a yes.
  • Keep private details out. First initials and ages are plenty. Leave out full names, addresses and anything you wouldn’t hand a babysitter.

Planning Thanksgiving With AI: Frequently Asked Questions

How far ahead should I start planning Thanksgiving?

Three to four weeks is comfortable, but the method works even a week out. The method starts with the headcount, then the menu and the turkey order about two weeks out, the shopping in three trips and the cooking timeline the week of. If you’re starting late, tell Claude how many days you have, and Claude will compress the plan.

Can I use this prompt in ChatGPT or other AI tools?

Yes. The ai.moms™ Thanksgiving Method is written in the RCTF format, so the prompt works in ChatGPT, Gemini and other AI tools as well as Claude. Paste the prompt into a Project or a new chat, turn on web search so the AI can look up store deadlines and prices, and answer the AI’s questions as you go.

Can AI make a Thanksgiving cooking schedule?

Yes. Tell Claude your serving time, your ovens and burners and every dish on the menu, and ask Claude to work backwards from mealtime. Claude will move what it can to the slow cooker, Instant Pot, air fryer or microwave to free up the oven.

Does this work if we’re not hosting?

Yes. Step 1 asks whether you’re hosting, going to someone else’s house or splitting the day. If you’re a guest, Claude plans your dish, your travel and the kids’ bag instead of the whole meal.
